DesignArena, and what it means for this bench

DesignArena ranks models, coding agents and app builders by blind head-to-head votes from its users, on whatever they asked to build. This bench asks a question DesignArena never poses: does an agent build with your design system's components and tokens, and how much does your agent context help? Below: every instrument DesignArena uses, its 31 voted categories with today's numbers, a side-by-side comparison, and ten things worth adopting, adapting or leaving alone.

DesignArena tells you which model has the best taste on a blank canvas. It cannot tell you whether that model will respect your system, and its own data suggests the top-voted model is among the least likely to.

The model voters pick most often on image-to-website prompts ranks 31st of 35 at following the reference it was given. See M7.

The short answer

A preference arena, not a correctness benchmark

Users vote blind between two rendered outputs, and a Bradley-Terry fit turns the votes into an Elo-style rating per board. Everything else DesignArena runs (LLM rubrics, Micro Evals, usage panels, agent-behavior metrics, price and speed charts, one-off studies) is supplementary and stays out of the rating.

It measures taste on a blank canvas

The UI Component arena asks for a polished component in one HTML file, with Tailwind or any CDN library. No board lets a team plug in its own design system, so none can say whether a model would use it.

Its own data says voters reward ignoring the input

On image-to-website prompts, the most-preferred model follows its reference image less than 30 of 34 others, and each GPT release followed references less than the last. A design-system benchmark needs the opposite behavior, so arena rank is a weak proxy for design-system fit.

One number hides the failure mode, there too

On the Full Stack board, DesignArena's LLM rubric and its votes agree across all 49 models (Spearman 0.71) but not where a choice is made: 0.01 among the top 15 and -0.46 among the top 10. That is this bench's case for gating on the worst dimension, made with their data.

What this bench lacks is mostly rendered output and statistics

DesignArena judges what renders, attaches sample floors and errors to ratings, plots quality against price and time, records agent behavior, and draws tasks from real requests. Each maps onto a planned item (D1, B2, B1, E5) or a small new info-only measure.

What DesignArena lacks is everything this bench is for

No ground truth, no hallucination or token checks, no accessibility measure, no compile gate, no context-level lift, no CI, and no way for an outsider to rerun a number.

How DesignArena measures

Seven instruments. M1 and M2 produce every leaderboard rating; M5 to M7 are published beside the ratings, never folded into them.

M1
Applies to
every board

Tournament

Session
A user picks a category, or a router assigns one from the prompt, then submits a prompt. Single-shot arenas draw four models plus a backup; agent and builder arenas pair two.
Votes
Two pairs are shown side by side without names; the winners meet, the losers meet, and a tie-breaker settles the middle. Five votes and a full 1st to 4th ordering per session. The mobile arena also accepts a tie.
Bias controls
Identities hidden until after the vote, left and right randomized, both outputs revealed together so speed does not leak, captchas at launch to keep voters human, LLM moderation of prompts. Model selection uses active sampling toward informative pairings (the About page still calls it random), and the first two models to finish are the ones shown.
M2
Applies to
every board

Rating

Model
Bradley-Terry strengths fitted iteratively (tolerance 0.0001, at most 200 iterations), reported as 400 × log10(strength) on an Elo-like scale anchored at 1200. Every vote weighs the same, with no filtering or editorial adjustment.
Floors
Under 15 comparisons a model is dropped before fitting; under 50 votes it carries a "new" tag; ratings count as preliminary until about 200 comparisons, varying by category.
Uncertainty
The methodology notes describe the margin of error as an approximate 95% Wilson interval on win rate; the leaderboard footnote calls it the Bradley-Terry standard error. The API returns that standard error for agent, video and image-editing boards (about 10 rating points at 1,770 battles, 4 at 12,000) and none for the single-shot HTML boards.
Cadence
Live results every two hours. New models usually appear within days of release; retirements are logged with a reason (superseded, or consistently bottom-ranked).
M3
Applies to
every board

Prompts

Source
Real user prompts up to 5,000 characters. Agentic web-app prompts are classified into 14 buckets; over the last 30 days the mix was productivity 22.5%, corporate 15.2%, e-commerce 13.4%, dashboards 11.3%, education 7.8% and landing pages 7.7%, with UI components at 2.6% and data viz at 0.4%.
Enhancer
An optional button has a low-cost model rewrite the prompt to two to four times its length, adding colors, layout, typography, accessibility and responsive details. The UI Component template even asks for "integration with design systems"; no system is ever supplied.
Moderation
A low-cost model flags profanity, sexual content, hate, threats and political content before anything is generated.
M4
Applies to
every board

Generation

Single-shot
Entry requires text in and one HTML, CSS and JavaScript file out. One system prompt per category, identical across providers; the UI Component prompt asks for a polished, production-ready component, with Tailwind, Heroicons or other CDN libraries allowed. Sampling and token limits follow each provider's defaults; effort levels such as xhigh and max are separate entries.
Agentic
Agent Runner, DesignArena's MIT-licensed, provider-agnostic Python harness: file tools, ripgrep search, bash behind a command filter, scaffolding, screenshots, image and video tools, Vercel deploy; a configurable round limit (50 in the example) and a 120 s tool timeout. Each track starts from a pre-baked template: React and TypeScript, Supabase and Vercel for full stack, Expo, Kotlin with Compose. Per-lab tuning exists and is private.
Builders
Commercial products (Lovable, Replit, v0, Figma Make, Bolt and others) get the same one-shot prompt with default settings and return a deployed URL.

Rubrics, Micro Evals and agent behavior

Full Stack rubric
An automated LLM rubric scores each app on eight criteria: schema, seed data, API, CRUD, auth, UI, end-to-end persistence and error handling. A separate 100-point schema rubric, GPT-5.2 reading the SQL, is stored per generation and kept out of the Elo.
Micro Evals
Binary, LLM-judged checks, each added only when a failure is both common in real use and discriminating. Seeded from Vercel's next-evals-oss plus failures seen on DesignArena, covering Next.js, Tailwind, Vercel deploys, Supabase, shadcn and TypeScript; reported as pass rates and a radar per agent.
Bash recovery
Failures per session, retries needed to recover, and the share of failures never recovered from (the agent gave up or looped).
Price, speed
Every board plots rating against price per million tokens (at 3:1 input to output) and against generation time.

Studies

Mirroring
25,000 websites that 35 models built from user reference images, compared pairwise by CLIP embedding (9.4 million pairs). The slope of website similarity on reference similarity is "mirroring"; shuffling the references flattens it to about zero, so it is real. GPT-6 Astra (max) is first by rating and 31st on mirroring (0.22 against a 0.27 median). Successive GPT releases went 0.37, 0.28, 0.25, 0.22 while their websites grew more alike whatever the input: a stronger house style.
Taste drift
Website styles ranked by the same votes from January to October 2026: near-black pages with one saturated accent gained, gold-and-serif luxury and earthy beige lost. The same site can win in January and lose in October. No figures published.
Judge checks
Microsoft Research Asia's OpenDesign (840 prompts, GPT-4o judging a rendered screenshot plus an interactive pass) reports 80.9% judge-human agreement and "broad agreement" with arena rankings, without a coefficient; DesignArena puts judge-human agreement at 60 to 80%. The model trained on it, AesCoder-4B, was later retired as consistently outclassed.

The two instruments measure different things (the rubric leans on backend quality, the votes on what a voter sees and clicks), so some disagreement is expected. A human signal to calibrate the judge

They do
Votes are the primary signal and LLM rubrics stay supplementary. The OpenDesign work they publicized puts judge-human agreement between 60 and 80%.
Here
Judgment carries 0.30 of the composite and is entirely model-judged. Nobody has measured how often a design-system team agrees with it.

GitHub-first and offline, tested with synthetic votes; no new dependency, since Bradley-Terry is a short iterative fit. Calibrates the judge; never becomes the target.

Where this bench is ahead

The design system is the subject

Context levels (bare, AGENTS.md, skill) and paired Lift say whether a team's docs help agents. DesignArena holds context fixed and varies the model, so it cannot say whether your AGENTS.md is worth keeping.

Ground truth, extracted

Catalog and tokens come from the system's own source, so a hallucinated component, an invented prop, a raw hex value or a foreign UI import is a fact, not an opinion. Stale catalogs are refused.

Accessibility is graded

Eight AST rules with a configurable accessible-name vocabulary today, runtime checks planned. None of DesignArena's boards or panels read here measures accessibility.

Failure modes stay visible

Six dimensions and a worst-dimension gate, with "ignored the design system" as its own metric. DesignArena publishes one rating per board, and its own Full Stack data shows how that reorders the frontier.

Hidden ground truth

Prompts never name components, and a linter enforces it. DesignArena's enhancer does the reverse, writing concrete specifications into the prompt.

Reproducible and private

Frozen baselines, stored diffs re-graded with no model call, CI with coverage and inconclusive exits, all on the team's own machine. No outsider can rerun a DesignArena number: prompts are live, votes are private, per-lab agent configs are private.

House style, measured in code

The vocabulary check uses a lexicon of names models invented across 898 graded generations. It is the code-level counterpart of DesignArena's "projecting": what a model does when it falls back on its own habits.

Seconds, no key

The static audit scores AI-readiness with no model at all, and written reports must trace every figure to computed data. DesignArena has no equivalent of either.

What not to copy

A public per-model leaderboard

The roadmap rules it out: the bench ranks design systems, and model columns exist to show that a finding is not one model's quirk.

Crowd preference as the objective

Voters reward models that impose a house style over the input, and DesignArena's own essay on taste lists novelty-seeking votes, unrealistic prompts and low stakes for the grader as weaknesses. Use human votes to calibrate the judge (G2), not as the target.

One rating per board

Two instruments on the same Full Stack board order the top ten differently. Keep six dimensions and the gate.

Prompt enhancement

Rewriting prompts with design specifics would push expected components and styles into the task and break hidden ground truth.

A live task set in the gate

Live prompts keep DesignArena current and make months incomparable, and its own drift study shows the same output scoring differently by month. The gate needs a frozen suite; real-work prompts belong in an optional pack.

Pixel diffing

Still out of scope. If design input arrives (E2), an embedding-based fidelity signal like mirroring is the closer fit.

Open questions

  • Q1

    Human review lane (G2). Is a 50-vote designer session per run worth building, given it can only ever be small-n and info-only? It is the only route to a measured judge-human agreement for the dimension that carries 30% of the composite.

  • Q2

    Visual judge. Should D1 stop at DOM, runtime and accessibility-tree facts, or later add a vision-model rubric on screenshots in shadow mode? DesignArena and OpenDesign both rely on rendered judgments; Terra's review warns that a screenshot cannot carry DOM facts.

  • Q3

    Second agentic family (E1). Agent Runner gives one tool interface across many providers, but its default policy includes a shell. Adopt it under this bench's no-shell policy, or wait for another vendor CLI?

  • Q4

    Task mix (E5). Size product task packs by DesignArena's observed request mix, or by a team's own ticket history?
